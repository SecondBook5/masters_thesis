\chapter{StageBridge: Data and Methods}
\label{chap:methods}

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:methods-overview}

StageBridge estimates progression-aligned epithelial regulatory transport from
cross-sectional spatial transcriptomic data. The epithelial component decoded
from each retained spatial measurement is treated as a receiver characterized
by an observed regulatory state and an ecological context. Because source- and target-stage receivers are sampled from different tissue
sections and donors, direct receiver-level correspondences are unavailable. StageBridge therefore combines a probabilistic source--target coupling with a continuous model of regulatory state change.

Each receiver is represented by two distinct feature sets. The observed
receiver state, \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\), is derived from the epithelial
expression profile decoded from Visium spot \(i\) and comprises PROGENy pathway
activities and CollecTRI transcription-factor activities. The context vector,
\(w_i\), contains non-epithelial composition estimates from the focal spot
together with spatial neighborhood summaries constructed independently within
the corresponding tissue section. Epithelial abundance, epithelial expression,
and receiver-derived regulatory features are excluded from the context
representation before model fitting.

Source- and target-stage receivers are related through ecologically stratified entropic optimal transport. Broad non-epithelial composition is used to define coarse ecological strata with support across stages and donors,
and entropically regularized optimal transport is then computed within each retained stratum using distance in regulatory state space. This construction restricts correspondence to receivers occupying comparable broad ecological settings while reserving finer receiver-specific context for the dynamical model. The separation is intentional: if the transport coupling were optimized directly on the same context features whose contribution is subsequently
estimated, ecological information would be encoded in the correspondence upstream of the context-effect analysis.

Regulatory change is modeled along a normalized progression coordinate,
\(\tau \in [0,1]\), using a structured neural differential field. The field contains a self component conditioned on receiver state and progression
coordinate and a context component that additionally receives the ecological
representation. Training proceeds in three stages. Conditional flow matching
first estimates the self field, then estimates the context field while holding
the self field fixed, and finally refines both fields jointly under an anchoring penalty that
constrains the self component toward its first-stage estimate.

The primary estimand is the conditional context residual. For source receiver
\(i\), with observed regulatory state \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and ecological
context \(w_i\), the full and self-only fields are integrated from the same
initial condition to the target end of the progression interval:

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cond}}\!\left(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}},w_i\right)
=
z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(1)
-
z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(1).
\label{eq:methods-rcond-overview}
\end{equation}

Here, \(z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(1)\) and \(z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(1)\) denote the
regulatory endpoints obtained by integrating the full and self-only fields,
respectively, from \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\). The observed source context is held
fixed throughout integration of the full trajectory. Both endpoints and the
resulting residual are expressed directly in named regulatory coordinates
rather than in an unconstrained latent space; Section~\ref{sec:methods-estimand}
gives the full construction and its scope.

Model assessment includes
donor-held-out prediction, donor-level uncertainty estimation, substitution of
specimen-average context, matched context shuffling, architectural ablation, and
synthetic recovery benchmarks.

\section{Cohorts, progression edges, and analysis units}
\label{sec:methods-cohorts}

\subsection{Cohorts and progression edges}
\label{sec:methods-luad-cohort}
\label{sec:methods-panin-cohort}

The primary analysis used a lung adenocarcinoma progression atlas containing
paired single-nucleus RNA-sequencing and Visium spatial transcriptomic
measurements spanning normal lung, atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH),
adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), and invasive lung adenocarcinoma. The single-nucleus measurements provided the cell-state reference used for annotation, regulatory-state construction, and spatial deconvolution. The Visium measurements provided the spatial receivers used for transport modeling.

The principal model-development edge was AAH\(\rightarrow\)AIS, representing the transition from an early premalignant lesion to a preinvasive adenocarcinoma state. Additional ordered edges were analyzed only when both source and target stages retained sufficient support across patients and coarse ecological strata. Edge-specific datasets were defined before model fitting, and each model was trained and evaluated within a single ordered source--target contrast.

The secondary analysis used spatial transcriptomic sections containing graded pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia, with low-grade and high-grade PanIN defining the ordered transition. These data were processed through the same receiver-state construction, context construction, conditional transport, and dynamical modeling workflow used for LUAD. Disease-specific models were fitted independently: transport couplings, feature transformations, model parameters, and vector fields were estimated separately for the LUAD and PanIN cohorts.

Because the PanIN cohort was smaller, its cross-validation and ecological stratification schemes were reduced to preserve common support within the training folds. The PanIN analysis therefore served as a secondary evaluation of estimator behavior under reduced sample support and portability to a biologically distinct, fibroblast-rich ductal precursor system, not as a comparably powered discovery cohort.

\subsection{Spatial receiver definition}
\label{sec:methods-analysis-unit}

The StageBridge analysis unit is the epithelial component decoded from a retained Visium spot. Because each Visium spot aggregates transcripts from multiple cells, it constitutes a spatially mixed measurement rather than a single-cell observation. DestVI decoding, constructed in Section~\ref{sec:methods-destvi}, separates each spot into the receiver state and the ecological context.

A StageBridge receiver is therefore the inferred epithelial component of a spatial spot, not a dissociated epithelial cell or single nucleus. The single-nucleus measurements served as the reference for deconvolution and cell-state annotation and as the substrate for secondary co-expression analyses; they were not used as transport receivers.

For receiver \(i\), \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) denotes the observed epithelial
regulatory state, \(w_i\) the ecological context, \(d_i\) the donor identifier, and \(s_i\) the pathological stage. Donor identity defined the independent
biological unit for data partitioning and uncertainty estimation. All receivers
from a given donor were assigned to the same cross-validation fold, ensuring that every spatial measurement from that donor contributed either to model
fitting or to held-out evaluation within a given fold, but never to both.

Receiver and context representations were constructed independently and joined using a unique section-and-barcode identifier. Spatial measurements lacking a valid epithelial decode, a matched context record, or the minimum required epithelial abundance were excluded before downstream model fitting. These
filters were applied before construction of the final edge-specific receiver sets and cross-validation partitions.



\section{Construction of receiver and context representations}
\label{sec:methods-representations}

Each retained spatial measurement was assigned two independently constructed representations: an epithelial regulatory state derived from lineage-specific expression decoded at the focal spot, and an ecological context combining non-epithelial focal composition with spatial neighborhood structure from the corresponding tissue section. The two representations were indexed by a common section-and-barcode identifier and joined only after feature construction.

\subsection{Preprocessing, deconvolution, and epithelial decoding}
\label{sec:methods-spatial-preprocessing}
\label{sec:methods-destvi}

Spatial measurements were processed separately for each ordered progression edge while preserving section identity, donor identity, pathological stage, and two-dimensional Visium coordinates. Spots with fewer than \(100\) detected genes and genes detected in fewer than \(3\) spots were removed. Counts were
library-size normalized to \(10^{4}\) transcripts per spot and transformed as
\(\log(1+x)\), with the raw-count matrix retained for downstream procedures that required count-scale input. The \(2{,}000\) most highly variable genes were
selected from the raw-count layer using the Seurat v3 procedure and used for spatial neighborhood feature construction. All spatial graphs and neighborhood
features were computed independently within each tissue section. Complete preprocessing thresholds are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app-c-preproc}.

Cell-type composition and lineage-specific expression were estimated using DestVI \autocite{Lopez2022-ps}, a probabilistic deconvolution model that relates spatial transcriptomic measurements to a single-cell reference while allowing
continuous within-lineage variation. DestVI produces two outputs used separately by StageBridge: estimated cell-type proportions for each spot and a
decoded expression state for each represented lineage. The decoded epithelial profile defined the receiver state, whereas non-epithelial composition
contributed to the ecological context.

For LUAD, the analysis used precomputed DestVI models referenced to the Lung
Cancer Atlas and their corresponding spot-level composition estimates
\autocite{Salcher2022-pu}. Epithelial expression was reconstructed using the
decoder associated with each spatial sample. The primary LUAD analysis used the pulmonary alveolar type 2 lineage, whereas the PanIN analysis used the
disease-specific epithelial lineage defined by the pancreatic reference
annotation. Because sample-specific DestVI models were fitted independently, decoded expression matrices were restricted to the intersection of genes represented across all compatible decoders. This placed all retained receivers
within a common measured-gene space before regulatory-state construction.

The epithelial abundance assigned to spot \(i\) was defined as

\begin{equation}
a_i^{\mathrm{epi}}
=
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{E}}
\widehat{\pi}_{i\ell},
\label{eq:methods-epi-abundance}
\end{equation}

where \(\widehat{\pi}_{i\ell}\) denotes the DestVI-estimated proportion of epithelial reference population \(\ell\), and \(\mathcal{E}\) is the disease-specific set of epithelial reference populations. A spot was retained as a receiver when \(a_i^{\mathrm{epi}} \geq 0.30\). The continuous abundance
estimate was preserved for threshold-sensitivity analyses. Counts of decoded, context-matched, and abundance-filtered spots were recorded for each progression edge.

\subsection{Regulatory receiver state}
\label{sec:methods-receiver-state}

Decoded epithelial expression was transformed to the library-normalized logarithmic scale used for regulatory activity inference:

\begin{equation}
x_{ig}^{\mathrm{log}}
=
\log\!\left(
1+10^{4}\widetilde{x}_{ig}^{(e)}
\right),
\label{eq:methods-decoded-log}
\end{equation}

where \(\widetilde{x}_{ig}^{(e)}\) is the expected expression of gene \(g\) assigned to epithelial lineage \(e\) in receiver \(i\).

Regulatory activities were inferred with the decoupleR univariate linear model
\autocite{Badia-I-Mompel2022-te}. This procedure estimates regulator activity from coordinated expression of weighted target genes and therefore produces activity scores rather than measurements of regulator expression. That
distinction is fundamental to the interpretation of the conditional context residual and is formalized in Section~\ref{sec:methods-estimand-scope}. PROGENy supplied the weighted pathway--target networks \autocite{Schubert2018-ph}, and CollecTRI supplied the
curated transcription-factor--target relationships
\autocite{Muller-Dott2023-mu}.

For receiver \(i\), the observed regulatory state was

\begin{equation}
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
p_i \\
t_i
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_z},
\qquad
d_z = 14 + 759 = 773,
\label{eq:methods-receiver-state}
\end{equation}

where \(p_i\) contains PROGENy pathway activities and \(t_i\) contains CollecTRI transcription-factor activities. The stated dimensionality corresponds to the frozen LUAD analysis matrix. Regulatory coordinates were
ordered consistently across samples and cross-validation folds. The same
scoring procedure was applied to each disease system, with disease-specific models retaining the coordinates represented in the corresponding frozen
analysis matrix.

The regulatory state space served three functions. It defined the coordinates in which the differential model generated trajectories, the cost space used to
construct source--target transport couplings, and the interpretation space in which conditional context residuals were summarized. Receiver states were constructed exclusively from decoded epithelial expression. Whole-spot regulatory scores and non-epithelial expression were excluded from the receiver representation.

\subsection{Ecological context and leakage control}
\label{sec:methods-context-construction}
\label{sec:methods-context-audit}

The ecological context combined focal non-epithelial composition with spatial
neighborhood structure:

\begin{equation}
w_i
=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_i \\
h_i
\end{bmatrix}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_w},
\label{eq:methods-context-vector}
\end{equation}

where \(q_i\) contains non-epithelial DestVI proportions for the focal spot,
\(h_i\) contains spatial neighborhood features computed within the corresponding
tissue section, and \(d_w\) denotes the dimensionality of the retained context representation.

The focal composition block contained the original DestVI proportions assigned to immune, stromal, endothelial, and other non-epithelial reference populations. Broad cancer-associated fibroblast and immune fractions were also
derived by summing disease-specific reference populations. These aggregate fractions defined the coarse ecological variables used during stratified transport construction, whereas the underlying cell-population channels
remained available to the dynamical context field.

Spatial neighborhood features were constructed using BANKSY
\autocite{Singhal2024-rv}. BANKSY augments each focal measurement with
distance-weighted summaries of surrounding expression. Its neighborhood-mean component captures average local molecular composition, whereas its azimuthal component captures first-order directional variation around the focal measurement. A spatial graph was constructed independently within each tissue section using \(k_{\mathrm{geom}}=18\) geometric neighbors. BANKSY was applied
to the \(2{,}000\) highly variable genes with neighborhood weight
\(\lambda=0.8\) and maximum azimuthal-gradient order \(m=1\). The selected \(\lambda\) produced a neighborhood-dominant representation while retaining a downweighted focal contribution, and \(m=1\) preserved first-order directional structure. Sections with fewer than \(74\) retained spots were excluded from neighborhood construction and recorded in the processing audit.

Because the standard BANKSY representation retains a focal-expression component, the assembled context could preserve information correlated with the receiver itself. Leakage control therefore comprised both explicit feature exclusion and an empirical reconstruction audit.

First, all directly receiver-derived or outcome-identifying channels were removed before model fitting. Excluded variables comprised epithelial and malignant composition estimates, whole-spot PROGENy and CollecTRI activities, malignant-fraction and dominant-cell-type summaries, whole-spot compositional diversity, donor identity, pathological stage, and pathology- or region-derived annotations. The retained numerical context contained only non-epithelial composition variables and section-specific spatial neighborhood features. Identifiers were retained exclusively for indexing and data alignment. The complete retained feature set is provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-c-ctxfeatures}.

Second, the residual capacity of \(w_i\) to reconstruct epithelial state was quantified directly. A ridge-regression model with
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{ridge}}=1\) was fitted from ecological context \(w_i\) to observed epithelial state \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) using source-stage receivers from training donors and evaluated on source-stage receivers from held-out donors. Held-out multivariate \(R^2\) and receiver-level reconstruction errors were then compared with a matched control in which the rows of the held-out context matrix were permuted. Where at least two receivers were available, the permutation was constrained to be a derangement so that no receiver retained its original context assignment.

Because row permutation preserves the marginal context distribution while destroying receiver--context correspondence, the paired reconstruction-error comparison tests whether the retained context predicts the epithelial state of its assigned receiver beyond what is expected from ecological marginals alone.
Substantial receiver-state reconstruction would indicate circularity in the subsequent context-effect estimate. The results of this audit are reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.

Receiver states and ecological contexts were joined by exact spot identifier to form the model-ready dataset

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}
=
\left\{
\left(
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}},
w_i,
a_i^{\mathrm{epi}},
d_i,
s_i
\right)
\right\}_{i=1}^{N},
\label{eq:methods-modeling-dataset}
\end{equation}

where \(d_i\) and \(s_i\) denote donor and pathological stage, respectively.
Only spots represented in both the receiver-state and context tables and having non-missing abundance estimates were retained. Cross-validation folds were assigned after all filtering at the donor level, with stage balance preserved where possible. All receivers from the same donor were assigned to the same fold,

\begin{equation}
f(i)=f(j)
\qquad
\text{whenever}
\qquad
d_i=d_j,
\end{equation}

where \(f(i)\) denotes the fold assigned to receiver \(i\). The complete modeling inventory is reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-c-inventory}.

\section{Ecologically stratified source--target correspondence}
\label{sec:methods-coupling}

Source- and target-stage receivers are sampled from unpaired cross-sectional
populations, so a probabilistic correspondence is required before a transition
field can be learned. StageBridge constructs this correspondence using
ecologically stratified entropic optimal transport: candidate pairs are first
restricted to receivers occupying comparable coarse ecological states, and
transport is then computed within each supported stratum using distance in
epithelial regulatory-state space, reserving the higher-dimensional
receiver-specific context for the dynamical model.

For an ordered progression edge, let

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}
=
\left\{
i:s_i=s_{\mathrm{src}}
\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal{T}
=
\left\{
j:s_j=s_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\right\},
\label{eq:methods-source-target-sets}
\end{equation}

where \(s_{\mathrm{src}}\) and \(s_{\mathrm{tgt}}\) denote the ordered source
and target stages. Couplings were estimated independently within each
cross-validation fold using training donors only. A new transport plan was
constructed at every optimization step from a donor-balanced minibatch.

\subsection{Coarse ecological stratification}
\label{sec:methods-stratification}

Coarse ecological position was defined from broad non-epithelial composition,

\begin{equation}
u_i
=
\begin{bmatrix}
u_i^{\mathrm{CAF}} \\
u_i^{\mathrm{immune}}
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:methods-coarse-context}
\end{equation}

where \(u_i^{\mathrm{CAF}}\) and \(u_i^{\mathrm{immune}}\) are the aggregated
cancer-associated fibroblast and immune fractions for receiver \(i\). These
variables form a low-dimensional subset of the complete context vector \(w_i\).

A coarse ecological stratifier was fitted separately within each training fold.
The coarse-context matrix was standardized feature-wise, principal-component
analysis was fitted to the standardized training matrix, and the first
component defined a scalar ecological score,

\begin{equation}
\eta_i
=
b_1^{\mathsf T}
\left[
\left(
u_i-\mu_u
\right)
\oslash
\sigma_u
\right],
\label{eq:methods-niche-score}
\end{equation}

where \(b_1\) is the first principal-component loading vector, \(\mu_u\) and
\(\sigma_u\) are the training-fold feature-wise mean and standard deviation,
and \(\oslash\) denotes element-wise division. Training-fold quantiles of
\(\eta_i\) then defined the discrete stratum assignment

\begin{equation}
c_i
=
a(u_i)
\in
\left\{
1,\ldots,K
\right\},
\label{eq:methods-stratum-assignment}
\end{equation}

where \(a\) denotes the complete fold-fitted transformation from coarse
composition to ecological stratum. The LUAD analysis used \(K=3\), whereas the
smaller PanIN analysis used \(K=2\).

Standardization parameters, the PCA loading, and quantile boundaries were
frozen after fitting and applied unchanged to source-stage, target-stage, and
held-out receivers. Stage, donor identity, epithelial abundance, epithelial
regulatory state, pathology-derived annotations, and the higher-dimensional
neighborhood representation were excluded from \(a(u_i)\). The resulting
strata therefore encode broad non-epithelial composition rather than
progression stage or receiver identity.

\subsection{Cross-stage common support and minibatch construction}
\label{sec:methods-common-support}
\label{sec:methods-coupling-minibatch}

Transport was restricted to ecological strata represented by sufficient
receivers and donors at both ends of the progression edge. For stratum \(c\),
let \(\mathcal{S}_c\) and \(\mathcal{T}_c\) denote the corresponding source-
and target-stage receiver sets. A stratum was eligible when

\begin{equation}
\min\!\left(
\left|\mathcal{S}_c\right|,
\left|\mathcal{T}_c\right|
\right)
\geq
n_{\min},
\qquad
\min\!\left(
\left|\left\{d_i:i\in\mathcal{S}_c\right\}\right|,
\left|\left\{d_j:j\in\mathcal{T}_c\right\}\right|
\right)
\geq
D_{\min},
\label{eq:methods-common-support}
\end{equation}

where \(n_{\min}\) is the minimum number of receivers and \(D_{\min}\) the
minimum number of distinct donors required from each stage. The evaluated
configuration used \(n_{\min}=10\) and \(D_{\min}=2\). This criterion prevented
correspondence from being defined within ecological settings represented
predominantly by one stage or one donor.

At each optimization step, source- and target-stage donors were sampled
separately from the training partition, and receivers were subsampled within
donor to form coupling sets of at most \(B_{\mathrm{OT}}=512\) receivers per
stage. The frozen stratifier assigned each sampled receiver to a stratum, and
one stratum satisfying Equation~\ref{eq:methods-common-support} was selected.
Batches without an eligible stratum were discarded and resampled. This
procedure bounded the quadratic memory cost of the transport matrix and reduced
the influence of donors contributing disproportionately many retained spots.

\subsection{Entropic optimal transport within stratum}
\label{sec:methods-entropic-ot}

Within the selected stratum, the cost of associating source receiver \(i\) with
target receiver \(j\) was the squared Euclidean distance between their
epithelial regulatory states,

\begin{equation}
C_{ij}
=
\left\|
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
-
z_j^{\mathrm{obs}}
\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:methods-ot-cost}
\end{equation}

The complete ecological contexts \(w_i\) and \(w_j\) were retained for
dynamical modeling but did not enter the within-stratum transport cost.

Let \(n_c\) and \(m_c\) denote the numbers of source and target receivers in
the selected stratum, with uniform empirical weights
\(\alpha_i=1/n_c\) and \(\beta_j=1/m_c\). Let
\(\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\beta)\) denote the set of nonnegative
\(n_c\times m_c\) transport plans with those marginals. The entropically
regularized coupling was

\begin{equation}
\Pi_c^{\ast}
=
\arg\min_{\Pi\in\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\beta)}
\left[
\sum_{i,j}
\Pi_{ij}C_{ij}
+
\varepsilon
\sum_{i,j}
\Pi_{ij}
\left(
\log\Pi_{ij}-1
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:methods-entropic-coupling}
\end{equation}

The transport term favors correspondences between nearby regulatory states,
whereas the entropy term distributes mass across alternative plausible
assignments. The evaluated configuration used \(\varepsilon=0.05\) and
\(80\) Sinkhorn iterations \autocite{Cuturi2013-li}. The Sinkhorn fixed point
and its log-domain implementation are derived in
Appendix~\ref{chap:appendix-a}.

Because the entries of \(\Pi_c^\ast\) sum to one, the fitted coupling defines a
categorical distribution over source--target pairs. At each optimization step,
\(64\) pairs were sampled according to

\begin{equation}
(i,j)
\sim
\operatorname{Categorical}
\left(
\operatorname{vec}
\left[
\Pi_c^{\ast}
\right]
\right).
\label{eq:methods-coupling-pair-sampling}
\end{equation}

High-mass correspondences were therefore sampled more frequently while
uncertainty over alternative target assignments was retained. For each sampled
pair, the source and target states, source and target contexts, and donor
metadata were preserved, and the regulatory displacement was defined as

\begin{equation}
\Delta z_{ij}
=
z_j^{\mathrm{obs}}
-
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_z}.
\label{eq:methods-pair-displacement}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Interpretation of the conditional coupling}
\label{sec:methods-coupling-interpretation}

The resulting plan defines probabilistic population correspondence within a
fold-specific domain of coarse ecological overlap. Broad composition determines
which receivers are eligible for comparison, and regulatory-state distance
determines the relative transport mass assigned within that domain. Fine
receiver-specific context remains available to explain variation in the learned
transition field without directly selecting the target assigned to each source
receiver.

These correspondences are model-defined alignments between cross-sectional
populations, not observed lineage relationships, clonal ancestry, or cell-level
transitions. Ecological adjustment is also limited to a discretized
one-dimensional projection of two broad composition variables. The conditional
context residual is therefore estimated after coarse ecological alignment
rather than exhaustive control of tissue ecology.


\section{StageBridge dynamical model}
\label{sec:methods-field}

StageBridge represents an ordered pathological edge as a continuous
transformation of epithelial regulatory state. Let
\(z(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\) denote regulatory state at normalized progression
coordinate \(\tau\in[0,1]\), and let \(w\in\mathbb{R}^{d_w}\) denote ecological
context. The source and target stages are assigned \(\tau=0\) and \(\tau=1\),
respectively. Intermediate values index positions along the modeled edge and
carry no chronological calibration.

\subsection{Field decomposition}
\label{sec:methods-field-decomposition}

The regulatory velocity is decomposed into receiver-state and
context-conditioned components,

\begin{equation}
\frac{dz(\tau)}{d\tau}
=
v_{\mathrm{full}}(z,w,\tau)
=
v_{\mathrm{self}}\!\left(z(\tau),\tau\right)
+
v_{\mathrm{context}}\!\left(z(\tau),w,\tau\right).
\label{eq:methods-stagebridge-field}
\end{equation}

The self field depends on the current regulatory state and progression
coordinate, whereas the context field additionally receives the ecological
representation. Both return velocities in the same regulatory space. This
additive decomposition defines the operational contrast underlying
\(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\). Because the two components are not identifiable from
endpoint fit alone, the staged optimization procedure in
Section~\ref{sec:methods-training} first estimates the self field before
introducing and jointly refining the context component.

\subsection{Receiver-state field}
\label{sec:methods-self-field}

The receiver-state field combines a capacity-constrained linear transition, an
explicit pathway mapping, and a nonlinear neural residual:

\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{self}}(z,\tau)
=
A\overline{z}
+
b
+
B\,r(z)
+
\rho_s(z,\tau).
\label{eq:methods-self-field}
\end{equation}

Here, \(r(z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d_r}\) extracts the \(d_r=14\) PROGENy pathway
coordinates, and \(B\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z\times d_r}\) maps those activities into
the complete regulatory velocity. The intercept
\(b\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\) permits state-independent drift, while
\(\rho_s:\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\)
captures nonlinear state-dependent velocity not represented by the structured
terms.

Direct pathway dependence would otherwise be represented redundantly by both
\(A\overline{z}\) and \(B\,r(z)\). Let
\(\mathcal{P}\subset\{1,\ldots,d_z\}\) denote the pathway-coordinate indices.
The state supplied to \(A\) was therefore masked as

\begin{equation}
\overline{z}_k
=
\begin{cases}
0, & k\in\mathcal{P},\\
z_k, & k\notin\mathcal{P}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:methods-pathway-mask}
\end{equation}

Thus, \(B\,r(z)\) represents direct linear dependence on pathway activity, whereas \(A\overline{z}\) acts on the transcription-factor coordinates. The neural residual receives the complete unmasked state and can represent nonlinear interactions across both blocks.

The linear operator was constrained to rank at most \(r_A=32\) through

\begin{equation}
A=UV,
\qquad
U\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z\times r_A},
\qquad
V\in\mathbb{R}^{r_A\times d_z},
\qquad
r_A=32.
\label{eq:methods-low-rank-a}
\end{equation}

This factorization limits linear-field capacity and represents shared modes of regulatory change within the \(d_z=773\)-dimensional state space. The factors serve as an optimization parameterization; interpretation is assigned to the composed operator \(A=UV\), not to the individually non-identifiable matrices
\(U\) and \(V\).

\subsection{Context-conditioned field}
\label{sec:methods-context-field}

The context-conditioned field allows identical epithelial states to receive different modeled velocities under different ecological representations:

\begin{equation}
v_{\mathrm{context}}(z,w,\tau)
=
M_{\mathrm{ctx}}
\left[
r(z)
\odot
\gamma(w)
\right]
+
\rho_c(z,w,\tau),
\qquad
\gamma(w)
=
\tanh(Gw+b_g).
\label{eq:methods-context-field}
\end{equation}

The gate
\(\gamma(w)\in[-1,1]^{d_r}\) maps ecological context to one bounded modulation score per pathway. Its element-wise interaction with \(r(z)\) conditions the structured contribution jointly on pathway activity and ecological state.
The matrix \(M_{\mathrm{ctx}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_z\times d_r}\) then maps the gated pathway representation into the complete regulatory velocity, while
\(\rho_c:\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_w}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d_z}\)
captures nonlinear context-conditioned velocity not represented by the pathway-gated term.

The structured context pathway is intentionally low dimensional: ecological information enters through \(14\) pathway channels before expansion to the full regulatory state. The \(759\) transcription-factor coordinates are not gated independently, although they may change through \(M_{\mathrm{ctx}}\) or through the unrestricted neural residual \(\rho_c\). Transcription-factor residuals therefore represent model-implied downstream displacement and cannot be uniquely attributed to direct ecological regulation of the corresponding factor or to the structured gate alone.

\subsection{Complete system and source-fixed context}
\label{sec:methods-source-context}
\label{sec:methods-complete-field}

Each trajectory was conditioned on the ecological context observed around its source receiver, held fixed over the modeled progression interval:

\begin{equation}
w_i(\tau)
=
w_i,
\qquad
\tau\in[0,1].
\label{eq:methods-source-fixed-context}
\end{equation}

Target context was retained with each transport-sampled pair but was not used by the evaluated model. Substituting the receiver-state and context-conditioned fields gives the complete initial-value problem:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dz_i(\tau)}{d\tau}
={}&
A\overline{z}_i(\tau)
+
b
+
B\,r\!\left(z_i(\tau)\right)
+
\rho_s\!\left(z_i(\tau),\tau\right)
\\
&+
M_{\mathrm{ctx}}
\left[
r\!\left(z_i(\tau)\right)
\odot
\tanh\!\left(Gw_i+b_g\right)
\right]
+
\rho_c\!\left(z_i(\tau),w_i,\tau\right),
\\
z_i(0)
={}&
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}},
\qquad
\tau\in[0,1].
\end{aligned}
}
\label{eq:methods-stagebridge-complete}
\end{equation}

The corresponding self-only trajectory was generated from the same observed initial state with the context field omitted:

\begin{equation}
\frac{dz_i^{\mathrm{self}}(\tau)}{d\tau}
=
v_{\mathrm{self}}
\left(
z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(\tau),\tau
\right),
\qquad
z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(0)
=
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\label{eq:methods-self-ivp}
\end{equation}

The full and self-only trajectories therefore differ through their integrated velocity fields. Context-dependent changes in early velocity alter subsequent states and later field evaluations, making their endpoint difference a trajectory-level contrast rather than a single-step perturbation.

StageBridge is a structured neural ordinary differential equation formulated in universal-differential-equation form \autocite{Rackauckas2020-mc}. The self--context decomposition, pathway masking, low-rank transition operator, and pathway-gated ecological interaction impose explicit model structure, while \(\rho_s\) and \(\rho_c\) provide flexible nonlinear approximation. These components organize the learned dynamics but do not constitute a calibrated biochemical kinetic model.


\section{Three-stage optimal-transport conditional flow matching}
\label{sec:methods-training}

Ecologically stratified optimal transport supplies probabilistic
source--target correspondences, and conditional flow matching converts those
correspondences into local supervision for the regulatory velocity field
\autocite{Lipman2022-ns,Bunne2022-go}. For a transport-sampled pair
\((z_0,z_1)\), a progression coordinate
\(\tau\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)\) defines the linear interpolation

\begin{equation}
z_\tau=(1-\tau)z_0+\tau z_1.
\label{eq:methods-cfm-interpolant}
\end{equation}

The model is evaluated at \(z_\tau\) and trained to predict the corresponding
source--target displacement:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau,\,(z_0,z_1)}
\left\lVert
v_{\theta}(z_\tau,w_0,\tau)
-
(z_1-z_0)
\right\rVert_2^2,
\label{eq:methods-general-cfm-loss}
\end{equation}

where \(w_0\) is the source-receiver context, held fixed along the interpolating
path. The transport plan was re-estimated at every optimization step, so
velocity supervision was sampled continuously from the fold-specific coupling
rather than from a fixed set of pairs. All receiver representations,
stratifiers, couplings, and model parameters were estimated using training
donors only.

\paragraph{Three-stage schedule.}
The self and context fields were estimated sequentially to define an operational
decomposition that cannot be recovered from endpoint fit alone. Stage A fitted
the receiver-state field without ecological input. Stage B froze that solution
and fitted the context field against the established self-field baseline. Stage
C jointly refined both components under an anchoring penalty that constrained
the self field toward its Stage A estimate. This schedule limits reassignment of
intrinsic progression to the context branch during joint optimization.

\paragraph{Composite objective.}
The Stage C objective contains all terms used during training:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}^{(\mathrm{C})}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}}^{(\mathrm{C})}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{endpoint}}^{(\mathrm{C})}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{residual}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{residual}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{sparse}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sparse}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{anchor}},
\label{eq:methods-stage-c-objective}
\end{equation}

with

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\mathrm{endpoint}}=0.25,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{residual}}=0.01,
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{sparse}}=10^{-4},
\qquad
\lambda_{\mathrm{anchor}}=0.1.
\label{eq:methods-training-loss-weights}
\end{equation}

Stage A used the self-field flow-matching and endpoint terms. Stage B optimized
the context field with the self field frozen and included the residual and
sparsity penalties. Stage C added the anchor term and jointly refined the full
field. The flow-matching loss supplied the primary local velocity supervision;
the endpoint loss penalized accumulated integration error at \(\tau=1\); the
residual and sparsity terms constrained context-conditioned velocity; and the
anchor preserved the Stage A self-field solution during joint refinement.

\paragraph{Integration and optimization.}
Trajectories were integrated over \(\tau\in[0,1]\) using fixed-step
fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration. A fixed solver schedule held the number
of field evaluations constant across folds and falsification conditions, so
paired comparisons did not differ in endpoint-integration budget. Dependence on
this numerical choice was evaluated in the integration-control rung.

Parameters were optimized with Adam. Each step used \(B=64\) pairs sampled from
a newly estimated transport plan constructed from at most \(512\) source and
\(512\) target receivers. Gradient norms were clipped at \(10\). Minibatches
without a stratum satisfying cross-stage common support were resampled up to
\(20\) times; runs that remained unsupported were terminated and recorded as
having insufficient ecological overlap.

Random generators governing parameter initialization and donor, receiver, pair,
and progression-coordinate sampling were seeded by fold. Training and held-out
donor sets were verified to be disjoint before fitting. LUAD used five
donor-held-out folds and PanIN used three. Fitted parameters, Stage A anchors,
training histories, coupling dimensions, donor assignments, and frozen
ecological transformations were retained for out-of-fold evaluation. Complete
hyperparameters and seeds are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app-c-hyperparams}.


\section{Conditional context residual}
\label{sec:methods-estimand}

For each source receiver, the fitted model generates a full trajectory under
the combined self and context fields and a self-only trajectory under the
fitted self field. Both begin from the same observed epithelial regulatory
state. Their endpoint difference defines the conditional context residual.

\subsection{Per-receiver estimand and out-of-fold calculation}
\label{sec:methods-estimand-receiver}
\label{sec:methods-estimand-out-of-fold}

For source receiver \(i\in\mathcal{S}\), let
\(z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(1)\) and \(z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(1)\) denote the endpoints
of the full and self-only initial-value problems. All evaluated analyses used
the identity endpoint readout, so the residual was calculated directly in the
annotated regulatory state space:

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cond},i}
=
z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(1)
-
z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(1)
\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_z}.
\label{eq:methods-rcond-identity}
\end{equation}

Both integrations used \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) as their initial condition. The
full trajectory received the observed source context \(w_i\), held fixed
throughout integration, whereas the self-only trajectory used the same fitted
self-field parameters with the context field omitted. For regulatory coordinate
\(k\),

\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{cond},ik}
=
z_{ik}^{\mathrm{full}}(1)
-
z_{ik}^{\mathrm{self}}(1).
\label{eq:methods-rcond-coordinate}
\end{equation}

Positive values indicate that activation of the fitted context branch increases
the predicted endpoint coordinate relative to the self-only trajectory, and
negative values indicate the converse.

All residuals used for cohort-level inference were computed out of fold. Each
receiver was evaluated using the model fitted without its donor; that donor
contributed neither to stratifier fitting, transport construction, parameter
optimization, nor model selection for the corresponding prediction.

\subsection{Trajectory-integrated interpretation}
\label{sec:methods-estimand-trajectory}

Because the two trajectories share their initial condition, their endpoint
difference can be written as the difference between their integrated
velocities:

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{cond},i}
=
\int_{0}^{1}
\Big[
&
v_{\mathrm{context}}
\left(
z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(\tau),
w_i,
\tau
\right)
\\
&+
v_{\mathrm{self}}
\left(
z_i^{\mathrm{full}}(\tau),
\tau
\right)
-
v_{\mathrm{self}}
\left(
z_i^{\mathrm{self}}(\tau),
\tau
\right)
\Big]
\,d\tau.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods-rcond-decomposition}
\end{equation}

The first term is the context-conditioned velocity accumulated along the full
trajectory. The remaining difference arises because the context field changes
the states subsequently visited by the full trajectory, causing the shared self
field to be evaluated along a different path. The residual therefore measures
an integrated endpoint displacement associated with activating the context
branch, including its downstream effect on state-dependent self dynamics. It is
not equivalent to the instantaneous context velocity
\(v_{\mathrm{context}}(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}},w_i,0)\).

\subsection{Donor-level aggregation and estimand scope}
\label{sec:methods-estimand-donor-aggregation}
\label{sec:methods-estimand-scope}

Spatial receivers from the same donor are correlated. Cohort summaries were therefore constructed by averaging first within donor and then across donors with equal weight. Let
\(\mathcal{I}_d=\{i\in\mathcal{S}:d_i=d\}\),
\(N_d=|\mathcal{I}_d|\), and let
\(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eval}}\) denote the set of
\(D_{\mathrm{eval}}\) evaluated source-stage donors:

\begin{equation}
\overline{R}_{\mathrm{cond},d}
=
\frac{1}{N_d}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_d}
R_{\mathrm{cond},i},
\qquad
\overline{R}_{\mathrm{cond}}
=
\frac{1}{D_{\mathrm{eval}}}
\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{eval}}}
\overline{R}_{\mathrm{cond},d}.
\label{eq:methods-rcond-aggregation}
\end{equation}

Thus, donors rather than spatial measurements determine the weighting of cohort-level residuals.

The estimand's value is conditional on the receiver and context representations, transport-defined correspondence, trained differential field, source-fixed context convention, and
common-support population. Because the coordinates are inferred regulatory activities, a residual assigned to a pathway or transcription factor denotes displacement in its transcriptional activity footprint rather than a change in regulator abundance,  dosage, or mutational status. The estimand is a within-model trajectory contrast; it does not identify historical lineage or a causal effect of experimentally manipulating tissue context.

\section{Evaluation and falsification framework}
\label{sec:methods-evaluation}
\label{sec:hypotheses}

Evaluation was organized around the three hypotheses introduced in Chapter~1.
Each is restated here in the notation of this chapter and paired with its
criterion for support; Section~\ref{sec:methods-program-stability} formalizes
H1, Section~\ref{sec:methods-context-controls} formalizes H2, and
Section~\ref{sec:methods-evaluation-scope} states the H3 procedure.

\paragraph{H1 --- a reproducible conditional context residual exists.}
For at least one regulatory coordinate $k \in \{1,\ldots,d_z\}$, the equal-donor
cohort residual $\overline{R}_{\mathrm{cond},k}$ of
Equation~\ref{eq:methods-rcond-aggregation} has the same nonzero sign in every
evaluated fold, with fold-specific bootstrap interval excluding zero. This is
the \emph{stable} criterion of Section~\ref{sec:methods-program-stability}
applied coordinate-wise.

\paragraph{H2 --- that information is receiver-local.}
Writing $D_\bullet$ for the held-out conditional endpoint distance of
Equation~\ref{eq:methods-conditional-endpoint-cost} evaluated under a named
context condition (so $D_{\mathrm{local}}$, $D_{\mathrm{self}}$,
$D_{\mathrm{donor\text{-}avg}}$, and $D_{\mathrm{shuffle}}$ denote that same
quantity computed with the receiver-specific, self-only, donor--stage-average,
and shuffled context conditions respectively), H2 requires

\[
D_{\mathrm{local}} < D_{\mathrm{donor\text{-}avg}} < D_{\mathrm{self}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_{\mathrm{local}} < D_{\mathrm{shuffle}},
\]

reproduced as Equation~\ref{eq:h1-local} in
Section~\ref{sec:methods-context-controls}, which also specifies how each
condition is constructed.

\paragraph{H3 --- the estimand transfers across precursor systems.}
H3, stated in Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}, is evaluated by applying the estimand
unchanged to the independently fitted PanIN cohort
(Section~\ref{sec:methods-cohorts}); Section~\ref{sec:methods-evaluation-scope}
states the procedure in full. Because the PanIN cohort is smaller and not
comparably powered, H3 is a test of estimator behavior rather than independent
validation of the LUAD findings.

These three criteria were addressed through donor-held-out endpoint
prediction, donor-level uncertainty estimation, context substitution and
shuffling, model-component controls, transport diagnostics, and synthetic
recovery benchmarks.

The integrated field was first required to reproduce the held-out target-stage
population within the same ecological common-support domain used for model
construction. Conditional residuals were then evaluated for donor-level
uncertainty and reproducibility across independently fitted folds. Context
substitution and shuffling tested the spatial scale and receiver specificity of
the ecological signal. Additional controls challenged the coupling, numerical
integration, architecture, representation, and fitted field decomposition.
Transport-stability and synthetic-identifiability analyses defined the
conditions under which the inferred correspondence and resulting estimand
remained interpretable.

All primary real-data evaluations were performed on donors excluded from model
fitting, using five donor-held-out folds for LUAD and three for PanIN. Within
each fold, feature transformations, ecological strata, transport couplings,
Stage A anchors, and model parameters were estimated exclusively from training
donors.

\subsection{Donor-held-out endpoint agreement}
\label{sec:methods-heldout-endpoints}

For held-out fold \(f\), let \(\mathcal{S}_f\) and \(\mathcal{T}_f\) denote
the source- and target-stage receivers assigned to that fold. The model trained
without fold \(f\) generated a full-field endpoint
\(\widehat{z}_{i,1}^{\,[-f]}\) for every held-out source receiver, yielding the
predicted endpoint population \(\widehat{Z}_{1,f}\) and observed target
population \(Z_{\mathrm{tgt},f}\).

Primary endpoint evaluation preserved the ecological conditioning used to
define the estimand. The fold-specific stratifier fitted on training donors was
reloaded without refitting and applied to the held-out source and target
receivers. A stratum entered evaluation when it contained at least five
held-out source and five held-out target receivers, defining the held-out
common-support set \(\mathcal{C}_f^{\mathrm{eval}}\). For each
\(c\in\mathcal{C}_f^{\mathrm{eval}}\), the stratum-specific endpoint cost was

\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{OT},fc}
=
\operatorname{Sinkhorn}_{\varepsilon}
\left(
\widehat{Z}_{1,f}^{(c)},
Z_{\mathrm{tgt},f}^{(c)}
\right),
\label{eq:methods-stratum-endpoint-cost}
\end{equation}

and the conditional endpoint metric was the source-mass-weighted aggregate

\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{OT},f}^{\mathrm{conditional}}
=
\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_f^{\mathrm{eval}}}
\omega_{fc}
D_{\mathrm{OT},fc},
\qquad
\omega_{fc}
=
\frac{
n_{fc}^{\mathrm{src}}
}{
\sum_{c'\in\mathcal{C}_f^{\mathrm{eval}}}
n_{fc'}^{\mathrm{src}}
},
\label{eq:methods-conditional-endpoint-cost}
\end{equation}

where \(n_{fc}^{\mathrm{src}}\) is the number of held-out source receivers in
stratum \(c\). The operator
\(\operatorname{Sinkhorn}_{\varepsilon}\) denotes squared-Euclidean transport
cost under an entropically regularized coupling with uniform empirical weights.
Lower values indicate closer agreement between predicted and observed
target-stage populations. This metric was primary because it evaluates the same domain used to
estimate \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\).

Three unstratified quantities were retained as sensitivity summaries: global
Sinkhorn cost between \(\widehat{Z}_{1,f}\) and
\(Z_{\mathrm{tgt},f}\); energy distance, which compares cross-population
separation with the internal dispersion of each population; and the mean
probability assigned to the target class by a logistic classifier trained
within fold on observed source and target states. The classifier score was
descriptive and did not enter model fitting or selection.

Conditional and global transport calculations used
\(\varepsilon=0.05\) and \(80\) Sinkhorn iterations, with deterministic
subsampling to at most \(2{,}048\) receivers per population where required.
Energy-distance calculations used at most \(1{,}000\) receivers per
population. Complete metric definitions are provided in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app-b-metrics}.

\subsection{Donor-level uncertainty and cross-fold reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods-program-stability}

Uncertainty in \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\) was estimated at the donor level. Within
each held-out fold, receiver-level residuals were first averaged within donor,
producing one \(d_z\)-dimensional residual vector per evaluated source-stage
donor. Donors were then resampled with replacement \(200\) times. For fold
\(f\), containing \(D_f\) evaluated donors, bootstrap replicate \(b\) was

\begin{equation}
\overline{R}_{fk}^{\ast(b)}
=
\frac{1}{D_f}
\sum_{r=1}^{D_f}
\overline{R}_{\mathrm{cond},d_r^{\ast(b)}k},
\label{eq:methods-donor-bootstrap}
\end{equation}

where \(d_r^{\ast(b)}\) denotes the donor sampled at bootstrap position \(r\).
The \(2.5\)th and \(97.5\)th percentiles defined the fold-specific
\(95\%\) interval when at least two source-stage donors were available.

Program-level reproducibility was classified after all fold-specific estimates
were assembled. Let \(\mu_{fk}\) denote the equal-donor mean residual for
program \(k\) in fold \(f\), with bootstrap interval
\([L_{fk},U_{fk}]\). A program was classified as \emph{stable} when
\(\mu_{fk}\) had the same nonzero sign in every evaluated fold and every
fold-specific interval excluded zero; \emph{directionally stable} when the sign
was consistent across folds but at least one interval included zero; and
\emph{unstable} when the mean residual changed sign across folds.

The first hypothesis---that progression contains a reproducible conditional
context residual---was supported when at least one named regulatory program met
the stable criterion. This requirement evaluates reproducibility across
disjoint donor sets and independently fitted models. The three stability labels
are reproducibility classifications, not coordinate-wise
false-discovery-rate-controlled significance categories.

\subsection{Falsification ladder}
\label{sec:methods-context-controls}
\label{sec:methods-model-controls}

The falsification ladder evaluates the fitted system under conditions that
remove or replace one source of information at a time. Its primary ecological
contrast tests the second hypothesis: whether endpoint prediction depends on
the identity of the receiver's local context rather than only on intrinsic
state or broader specimen ecology.

Let \(D_{\bullet}\) denote the held-out conditional endpoint distance from
Equation~\ref{eq:methods-conditional-endpoint-cost} under the indicated
condition. Support for receiver-local ecological information required

\begin{equation}
D_{\mathrm{local}}
<
D_{\mathrm{donor\text{-}avg}}
<
D_{\mathrm{self}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
D_{\mathrm{local}}
<
D_{\mathrm{shuffle}},
\label{eq:h1-local}
\end{equation}

where \(D_{\mathrm{shuffle}}\) denotes the primary matched-state context
shuffle. The first chain tests whether receiver-specific context improves
prediction beyond donor--stage-average ecology and the self-only field. The
second tests whether improvement depends on the observed receiver--context
assignment rather than the marginal context distribution.

The components of the criterion were interpreted separately when the complete
condition was not met. Improvement over the self-only field without improvement
over donor--stage-average context indicated that ecological information was
recoverable primarily at specimen scale. Failure to distinguish local from
shuffled context indicated that receiver-specific neighborhood identity added
no detectable endpoint information beyond the ecological marginals preserved
by the shuffle.

The ecological arm therefore followed

\begin{equation}
\text{self-only}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{donor--stage-average context}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{receiver-specific context}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{shuffled context},
\label{eq:methods-context-ladder}
\end{equation}

while additional rungs challenged the coupling, numerical integration,
architecture, and fitted field components. Every condition was evaluated on
the same held-out fold using identical endpoint metrics, making each comparison
paired within the same biological source and target populations.

The fine-context shuffle reconstructed CAF/immune bins from the available
coarse-context columns rather than reusing the frozen fold-specific stratifier.
It is therefore an approximate broad-context-preserving perturbation rather
than an exact shuffle within the original transport strata.

Structured-only and neural-residuals-only conditions were applied after
training without refitting. They measure the contribution of fitted components
within the learned complete model, not the performance of models optimized
under those restrictions.

\subsection{Representation and decomposition credibility}
\label{sec:methods-credibility}

Two diagnostics evaluated whether the context-associated signal could be
explained by disguised receiver-state information or by dominance of the
unstructured neural residual.

The first was the context-to-state reconstruction audit described in
Section~\ref{sec:methods-context-audit}. Its reported quantities were held-out
multivariate \(R^2\) and receiver-level reconstruction error,

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MSE}_{\mathrm{recon}}
=
\frac{1}{N_f d_z}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_f}
\left\|
\widehat{z}_i
-
z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}
\right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:methods-context-reconstruction-mse}
\end{equation}

where \(\widehat{z}_i\) is the epithelial state predicted from \(w_i\) alone
and \(N_f=|\mathcal{S}_f|\). Observed-context reconstruction was compared with
the matched row-permutation control.

The second diagnostic compared the magnitude of the neural residual terms with
the structured field. At held-out source states and \(\tau=1\), define

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
v_{\mathrm{structured}}(z,w)
={}&
A\overline{z}
+
b
+
B\,r(z)
+
M_{\mathrm{ctx}}
\left[
r(z)
\odot
\tanh(Gw+b_g)
\right],
\\
v_{\mathrm{neural}}(z,w)
={}&
\rho_s(z,1)
+
\rho_c(z,w,1),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:methods-structured-neural-components}
\end{equation}

and the neural-residual dominance ratio

\begin{equation}
D_{\rho}
=
\frac{
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left\|
v_{\mathrm{neural}}(z,w)
\right\|_2^2
\right]
}{
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left\|
v_{\mathrm{structured}}(z,w)
\right\|_2^2
\right]
+
10^{-8}
}.
\label{eq:methods-d-rho}
\end{equation}

The prespecified descriptive regimes were
\(D_{\rho}\leq0.5\) for a structured regulatory-UDE regime,
\(0.5<D_{\rho}\leq1\) for a mixed structured-neural regime, and
\(D_{\rho}>1\) for a residual-dominated regime. Residual-dominated folds were
flagged as unsuitable for detailed interpretation of the structured
decomposition. The ratio therefore bounds the confidence assigned to
component-level or program-level attribution; it does not measure predictive
performance directly.

\subsection{Transport-target stability}
\label{sec:methods-transport-stability}

Transport-target stability quantified the sensitivity of inferred
correspondence to perturbation of the sampled target population. For source
state \(z_i\) and target subset \(A\), with entropic coupling
\(\Pi^{(A)}\), the barycentric target assigned to \(z_i\) was

\begin{equation}
T_A(z_i)
=
\frac{
\sum_j \Pi_{ij}^{(A)}z_j^{(A)}
}{
\sum_j \Pi_{ij}^{(A)}
}.
\label{eq:methods-barycentric-target}
\end{equation}

For independently sampled target subsets \(A\) and \(B\), the
matching-stability statistic was

\begin{equation}
D_N
=
\mathbb{E}_{A,B,\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{src}}}
\left[
\frac{1}{
\left|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{src}}\right|
}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{src}}}
\left\|
T_A(z_i)
-
T_B(z_i)
\right\|_2^2
\right],
\label{eq:methods-matching-stability}
\end{equation}

where \(\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{src}}\) is a sampled source batch. Lower
\(D_N\) indicates that independent target samples yield similar transport
destinations for the same source population.

The estimate used \(40\) independent draws, with source and target batches
capped at \(4{,}096\) receivers and each target subset containing half of the
available target population up to that cap. Because raw \(D_N\) depends on the
dimension and scale of the representation, two normalized quantities were also
reported:

\begin{equation}
D_N^{\mathrm{normalized}}
=
\frac{
D_N
}{
\operatorname{tr}
\left(
\widehat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tgt}}
\right)
},
\qquad
D_N^{\mathrm{per\ feature}}
=
\frac{D_N}{d},
\label{eq:methods-dn-normalized}
\end{equation}

where \(\widehat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tgt}}\) is the empirical target-state
covariance and \(d\) is the dimension of the evaluated representation. Within
each draw, the entropic coefficient was set to \(0.05\) times the median
squared-Euclidean cost of the sampled geometry to support comparison across
representations with different distance scales.

\subsection{Synthetic identifiability benchmark}
\label{sec:methods-synthetic-identifiability}

A synthetic benchmark tested whether alternative source--target correspondence
procedures could recover a known context displacement from unpaired
populations. The data-generating regimes and acceptance thresholds were fixed
before the selected coupling was applied to the biological cohorts, making the
benchmark a prospective screen for the primary design rather than a post-hoc
justification.

Synthetic source states were sampled from a multivariate Gaussian. Context
vectors were generated with configurable correlation \(\rho\) to the source
state, and a coarse niche class was formed by quantizing a one-dimensional
projection of context. The paired target state was

\begin{equation}
z_{1i}^{\mathrm{true}}
=
z_{0i}
+
T_{\mathrm{self}}(z_{0i})
+
R_i^{\mathrm{true}}
+
\epsilon_i,
\label{eq:methods-synthetic-dgp}
\end{equation}

where \(T_{\mathrm{self}}\) combined a stable linear transformation with a
small coordinate-wise nonlinear term,
\(R_i^{\mathrm{true}}\) was the planted context displacement, and
\(\epsilon_i\) was Gaussian noise with standard deviation \(0.1\). True
source--target identities were retained only for evaluation and for the oracle
ceiling. All non-oracle procedures received the target population after random
permutation.

Four correspondence procedures were compared: strict state-only transport,
state-only transport augmented by a population-level progression coordinate,
ecologically stratified transport using the coarse niche class, and oracle
pairing. Entropic plans were converted to one target assignment per source by
selecting the target receiving greatest coupling mass. The coupling-implied
displacement was then separated into a state-predictable component and a
residual.

Recovery was quantified by the median receiver-level cosine similarity between
the recovered residual \(\widehat{R}_i\) and
\(R_i^{\mathrm{true}}\), Pearson correlation after flattening the two residual
matrices, and the context-gain statistic

\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{context}}
=
\frac{
V_{\mathrm{self}}
-
V_{\mathrm{full}}
}{
V_{\mathrm{self}}
},
\label{eq:methods-synthetic-context-gain}
\end{equation}

where \(V_{\mathrm{self}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{full}}\) are the residual
variances of linear state-only and context-augmented displacement models.

The benchmark included a null regime with no context displacement; a supported
conditional regime in which context effects varied across an observed coarse
niche class; an orthogonal non-identifiable regime; a confounded-null regime
with state--context association but no planted context effect; and a sweep over
the alignment between context displacement and intrinsic progression.

Acceptance thresholds were prespecified. The null regime required
\(G_{\mathrm{context}}\leq0.10\). Recovery in the supported regime required
\(G_{\mathrm{context}}\geq0.15\), median cosine similarity
\(\geq0.50\), and flattened correlation \(\geq0.30\). The non-identifiable
regime required the prespecified non-oracle recovery statistic to remain below
\(0.15\). Oracle pairing defined the recoverability ceiling and was unavailable
to all biological analyses.

\subsection{Inferential scope}
\label{sec:methods-evaluation-scope}

The conditional within-stratum transport cost was the primary endpoint metric
because it evaluates predicted and observed populations within the same
coarse-ecological domain that defines \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\). Global transport
cost, energy distance, and target-stage classifier probability were retained as
secondary summaries.

Donor-bootstrap intervals quantify variation among biological donors, whereas
cross-fold classifications evaluate reproducibility across independently fitted
models and disjoint held-out donor sets. The \(d_z\) regulatory coordinates are
correlated outputs of one learned dynamical system. They were therefore
summarized through equal-donor means, effect magnitude, bootstrap intervals, and
cross-fold direction. Coordinate-wise \(P\)-values were not used to treat
pathway and transcription-factor outputs as independent confirmatory tests.
Stable, directionally stable, and unstable designations consequently describe
reproducibility rather than formal false-discovery-rate control.

Falsification conditions were compared with the receiver-specific context model
within fold using paired endpoint metrics. Mean differences and \(95\%\)
intervals across folds summarize the consistency of those paired contrasts;
cross-validation folds are prediction settings, not independent biological
replicates. The context-to-state audit, \(D_{\rho}\), and \(D_N\) bound the
credibility of the context representation, field decomposition, and transport
correspondence, respectively. The synthetic benchmark defines the recovery
boundary under controlled ground truth.

H3 (Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) was evaluated by applying the same receiver
construction, conditional residual, donor-held-out evaluation, and stability
criteria to the independently fitted PanIN cohort (Section~\ref{sec:methods-cohorts}).
Coherent behavior was defined as recovery of interpretable stability where the
cohort supported it; this served as a bounded test of estimator behavior under
a distinct precursor ecology and reduced sample support, not as independent
validation of the LUAD findings.



\section{Secondary biological analyses}
\label{sec:methods-biological-analyses}

The primary analyses estimated progression-aligned regulatory transport and
evaluated the conditional context residual through donor-held-out prediction
and prespecified falsification controls. Secondary analyses characterized the
observed biology of each progression edge, localized selected residuals within
the tissue environment, and generated regulatory and intercellular signaling
hypotheses. These analyses were performed after model fitting, without altering the
receiver representation, transport coupling, differential field, or definition
of \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\), and are organized below by their relationship to the
fitted model.
Stage-associated analyses use observed expression or spatial measurements and
are independent of StageBridge outputs. Residual-associated analyses use
out-of-fold \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\) estimates to characterize the ecological,
spatial, and geometric distribution of identified effects. Residual-guided
analyses use model outputs to prioritize candidate regulators and signaling
interactions and therefore generate mechanistic hypotheses rather than
independent validation. Complete software versions and parameter settings are
reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-c-software}.

\subsection{Stage-associated analyses}
\label{sec:methods-stage-transcriptomics}
\label{sec:methods-stage-discrimination}

Stage-associated differential expression and gene-set enrichment provided a
model-independent description of each ordered progression edge. These analyses
used the edge-specific, annotated, log-normalized single-nucleus object and did
not use transport pairs, ecological context, modeled trajectories, or
conditional residuals. Genes were ranked between target and source stages using
the Wilcoxon procedure implemented in
\texttt{scanpy.tl.rank\_genes\_groups}
\autocite{Wolf2018-rc}, with the target stage specified as the comparison
group so that positive statistics indicated higher target-stage expression.

The complete signed ranking was analyzed with decoupleR
\autocite{Badia-I-Mompel2022-te} against the MSigDB Hallmark and Gene Ontology
Biological Process collections
\autocite{Liberzon2015-cq}, using fgsea
for preranked enrichment \autocite{Korotkevich2016-ou}. Gene sets represented
by fewer than five ranked genes were excluded, and positive normalized
enrichment scores indicated enrichment toward the target stage.

Because individual nuclei rather than donors entered the differential-expression
ranking, these analyses were interpreted as descriptive characterizations of
the observed transition. Donor-held-out prediction and donor-level residual
summaries provided the primary inferential evidence for conclusions associated
with StageBridge.

Stage-discrimination analyses tested whether the epithelial regulatory state
contained a reproducible source--target signal before ecological context was
introduced. Only the PROGENy and CollecTRI activities comprising the receiver
representation were used. The principal analysis employed stage-stratified
donor-held-out cross-validation: donors were partitioned separately within
source and target stages so that each evaluable test fold contained both
classes while maintaining complete donor separation. The number of folds was
limited by the smaller stage-specific donor count.

Within each fold, regulatory features were standardized using training-donor
parameters, logistic regression was fitted to the standardized training data,
and the transformation and classifier were applied unchanged to held-out
donors. Discrimination was summarized by the area under the receiver-operating
characteristic curve averaged across evaluable folds.

Two sensitivity analyses were retained. A pooled receiver-level analysis
allowed measurements from the same donor to contribute to both training and
evaluation and therefore measured the combined stage- and donor-associated
signal present in the representation. A within-donor analysis was restricted
to donors containing both stages and tested stage discrimination while donor
identity was fixed. The three analyses were interpreted jointly to distinguish
generalizable stage structure from donor-specific orientation of the
regulatory state space.

\subsection{Residual-associated analyses}
\label{sec:methods-residual-localization}
\label{sec:methods-spatial-organization}
\label{sec:methods-projected-dynamics}

Out-of-fold receiver-level residuals were mapped to their original spatial
measurements and fold-specific ecological strata to determine where identified
regulatory programs were expressed most strongly within the supported
ecological domain. Residuals were first averaged within donor and stratum, after
which cohort summaries assigned equal weight to each donor represented in that
stratum. Strata were ordered by the fold-specific CAF/immune score used during
transport construction.

Monotonic association between stratum order and
\(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\) was quantified by Spearman correlation. Significance was
evaluated using \(2{,}000\) within-donor permutations of stratum labels.
Permuting within donor preserved each donor's residual distribution and stratum
counts while disrupting their observed correspondence, an important constraint
because the same strata contributed to transport construction. Two-sided
empirical \(P\)-values were calculated as the fraction of permuted correlations
whose absolute value equaled or exceeded that of the observed statistic. These
analyses were restricted to programs selected from the out-of-fold residual
results and characterized the ecological distribution of an identified effect;
they did not constitute an independent program-discovery procedure.

The spatial organization of ecological variables was assessed using the
complete context table joined to the original Visium coordinates. Spatial
graphs were constructed independently within each tissue section so that no
edge connected measurements from different specimens. A
\(15\)-nearest-neighbor graph was constructed for each section. Moran's \(I\)
\autocite{Moran1950-nc} quantified global spatial autocorrelation of
malignant, cancer-associated fibroblast, immune, and compositional-diversity
features. Getis--Ord \(G_i^\ast\) statistics
\autocite{Ord1995-pl} identified local concentrations, with
standardized values greater than \(1.96\) classified as hot spots and values
below \(-1.96\) as cold spots.

Spatial statistics were calculated separately for each section and summarized
by pathological stage, with tissue section as the independent unit for
stage-level comparisons. Spearman correlation tested monotonic association with
ordered stage, Kruskal--Wallis tests compared section-level values across more
than two stages, and prespecified two-stage comparisons used two-sided
Mann--Whitney \(U\) tests.

Observed ecological variance was additionally partitioned across pathological
stage, donor within stage, and receiver-level variation within donor. These
fractions described the scale at which each composition feature varied across
the cohort and were used to distinguish specimen-level ecology from local
spatial heterogeneity. They were not interpreted as causal variance
components.

Projected-field analyses visualized the geometry of the learned transition and
compared the gradient and rotational components of the self-only and complete
fields. Source and target regulatory states were embedded jointly in two
dimensions using PHATE \autocite{Moon2019-cb}, with UMAP
\autocite{McInnes2018-fb} and PCA retained as alternative representations.
Source-state velocities were estimated from the first displacement of a
multistep RK4 trajectory, projected into the shared embedding, and smoothed over
a regular grid.

A spectral Helmholtz--Hodge decomposition separated the gridded velocity field
into gradient and rotational components. The rotational fraction was defined
as the norm of the rotational component divided by the norm of the complete
projected field and was calculated separately for the self-only and complete
fields. Because the decomposition depends on the embedding, smoothing grid, and
illustrative fold, the projected rotational fraction is an
embedding-dependent geometric diagnostic rather than a coordinate-invariant
property of the original \(773\)-dimensional field. Empirical
negative-log-density surfaces were used only to summarize the embedded receiver
distribution visually and were not interpreted as physical free-energy
landscapes. Projected-field analyses were exploratory and did not contribute to
model fitting, model selection, or calculation of \(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\).

\subsection{Residual-guided analyses}
\label{sec:methods-grn}
\label{sec:methods-communication}

Gene-regulatory network analyses examined whether model-associated programs were represented in complementary co-expression and motif-supported network
structures. De novo transcription-factor regulons were inferred using pySCENIC
\autocite{Aibar2017-kq}. GRNBoost2 inferred transcription-factor--target associations from expression covariation across raw epithelial single-nucleus counts, cisTarget motif resources pruned these associations to motif-supported regulons, and AUCell quantified regulon activity in individual nuclei.

Stage-specific regulon activity and network connectivity were compared
descriptively with the corresponding CollecTRI residuals. Enrichment of selected residual-associated transcription factors among highly connected pySCENIC regulators was evaluated using an upper-tail hypergeometric test, with the universe restricted to transcription factors represented in both the StageBridge state space and the inferred network.

Weighted gene co-expression modules were inferred using hdWGCNA
\autocite{Morabito2023-yq}. Metacells were formed within compatible donor,
stage, and cell-type groups. A weighted adjacency matrix was constructed from
gene--gene correlations under the selected soft-thresholding power and
transformed into a topological-overlap matrix. Hierarchical clustering with dynamic tree cutting identified preliminary modules, which were merged according to eigengene correlation. Module eigengenes summarized module
activity, while intramodular connectivity and eigengene-based membership identified hub genes. Gene Ontology Biological Process enrichment was performed
with fgsea \autocite{Korotkevich2016-ou}, with false-discovery-rate adjustment
across tested module--gene-set combinations.

Intercellular communication analysis generated candidate signaling routes linking non-epithelial sender populations to epithelial regulatory programs.
LIANA+ consensus analysis was performed separately for source and target stages
using the annotated cell populations represented in each edge
\autocite{Dimitrov2024-fb}. Sender population, receiver population, ligand,
receptor, stage, and consensus interaction evidence were retained, with the
modeled epithelial populations designated as the principal receiver
compartment.

Receptor-to-transcription-factor paths were assembled from directed signaling
relationships in OmniPath \autocite{Turei2026-bn}, and CollecTRI supplied the
transcription-factor--target relationships connecting intracellular paths to
terminal regulatory programs \autocite{Muller-Dott2023-mu}. Candidate chains
therefore linked a sender population and ligand to an epithelial receptor,
downstream regulator, and terminal program.

The analysis was restricted to terminal programs classified as stable or
directionally stable across folds and having an absolute cohort residual of at
least \(0.05\). The sign of the cohort residual annotated whether the terminal
program was higher or lower at the complete-field endpoint relative to the
self-only endpoint. LIANA+ interaction evidence, intracellular path support,
path length, and residual magnitude were retained as separate ranking
attributes rather than collapsed into a single evidential score.
Ollivier--Ricci curvature \autocite{Ollivier2009-oq} was calculated on the
resulting networks as an exploratory measure of bottleneck-like edges and was
used for candidate prioritization rather than confirmatory inference.

Because the terminal programs were selected using
\(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\), the communication analysis was explicitly
residual-guided. The resulting chains identify extracellular signaling routes
compatible with the fitted regulatory displacement and generate hypotheses for
experimental evaluation. They neither independently validate
\(R_{\mathrm{cond}}\) nor establish a causal ligand--receptor mechanism.

\subsection{Statistical reporting}
\label{sec:methods-secondary-statistics}

The donor was the independent biological unit for residual summaries.
Receiver-level analyses retained donor structure through donor-level
aggregation, donor-held-out evaluation, or within-donor permutation. Tissue sections were the independent units for stage-level spatial comparisons.

Differences between falsification-ladder conditions were calculated within fold using paired endpoint metrics and summarized across folds by mean differences and \(95\%\) confidence intervals. Cross-validation folds represented separately fitted prediction settings and were not treated as independent biological samples.

Spearman correlation was used for ordered ecological and pathological-stage associations. Kruskal--Wallis and Mann--Whitney \(U\) tests were used for nonparametric section-level comparisons, and hypergeometric tests were used for regulator-set overlap. Tests were two-sided except for explicitly directional enrichment procedures. Gene-set and module-enrichment analyses used the false-discovery-rate-adjusted \(P\)-values returned by their respective
implementations.

Secondary network, spatial, and projected-field analyses were interpreted as exploratory unless supported by an explicitly donor-aware, section-aware, permutation-based, or multiple-testing-adjusted procedure. Analyses unavailable
or incomplete in the evaluated workflow were excluded from quantitative
conclusions.
